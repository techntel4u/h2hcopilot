Section 1: Prompts to follow the live workshop10 prompts

Work through these together during the session. Everyone does the same prompts, whatever your role.

01Welcome message
Application: Draft a friendly, on-brand welcome message for visitors in seconds.
"Write a 3-sentence welcome message for visitors to Warwick Academic school visitor in the style of a cheerful tour guide"
02Weak (PACTS) Prompt
Application: Run this deliberately vague prompt first and note how much the AI has to guess: who is writing, which gate, when, and why. Then compare the result with the strong PACTS version in the next exercise.
"Write an email about the main gate being locked."
03Strong (PACTS) Prompt: Staff Notice Email
Application: The same task as the weak prompt, now with full PACTS structure. Every element the AI had to guess is spelled out, so you get a clear, reassuring staff notice with the key time in bold.
"[P] Act as a facilities administrator. [A] Draft an email notice [C] informing staff that the sports field gate will be locked at 4pm on Friday for contractor work. [T] Keep it clear, polite, and reassuring. [S] Format as 2 short paragraphs with key time in bold."
04Rewrite an Email Draft (PACTS)
Application: Paste an existing draft and have the AI rewrite it for tone and format, turning a plain notice into a warm, parent-friendly message.

First copy this draft email into Copilot:

"Subject: Lost Property Collection Dear Parent/Carer, We have some lost property items that may belong to your child. If you think any of these items belong to them, please come to the school reception to collect them. Lost property can be collected between 8:30am and 4:00pm on school days. Any unclaimed items will be donated or disposed of at the end of term. Kind regards, School Reception Team"

Then add the activity prompt:

"Act as a school receptionist (P). Rewrite this draft email (A) regarding lost property pick-up (C). Make it warm and helpful (T) in 3 concise bullet points (S)."

Everyday admin and operations tasks. Each one is written in the PACTS framework — Persona, Action, Context, Tone, Structure — so you can see the pattern and reuse it on your own work.

[T] Keep every version polite and professional, never blunt. [S] Present three clearly labelled versions, each under 100 words: [paste email]"

Then paste this sample email:

18Rehearse a Difficult Conversation (PACTS)
Application: Useful for learning, or to prepare for an upcoming meeting. Rehearse performance reviews or conflict resolution in a safe space.
"[P] Act as a parent who is upset that their child’s after-school club has been cancelled at short notice. [A] Roleplay the conversation with me at the reception desk. [C] I am the administrator taking the complaint. The cancellation was unavoidable and there is no alternative session this term. Stay in character and wait for my reply each time. [T] Frustrated but never abusive. [S] After six exchanges, drop character and tell me three things I handled well and two I could improve."
Section 3: Additional Prompts11 prompts

Techniques and extras to explore in your own time — how Copilot remembers, how to chain prompts, and how to build a simple agent.

19Catch It Out (Breaks the PACTS Formula)
Application: A deliberate trap: this prompt breaks the PACTS formula and asks for a fiddly working-days calculation that depends on knowing the Bermuda public holidays in that period. The AI may miscount or miss a holiday while sounding completely confident. Run it, then check the answer against the official holiday dates — a reminder that confident-sounding output still needs verifying.
"Term 1 begins 2 September 2026. How many working days are there between 2 September and 20 December 2026, excluding weekends and any Bermuda public holidays in that period?"

Check the answer against the official list: Bermuda public holidays (gov.bm) ↗

20Copilot Memory
Set it once, use it everywhere: Copilot can hold your preferences across conversations. You set a rule a single time, and Copilot applies it to every chat that follows. No more re-typing the same setup whenever you open a fresh conversation.

To store a rule, type an instruction that begins with "Remember and save to memory". Copilot confirms it and keeps it.

To view your saved memories

  1. Click the three dots in the top right corner
  2. Click Settings
  3. Click Personalisation, then manage your saved memories

Three prompts worth saving

Regional preferences

Remember and save to memory that I prefer UK spelling, Bermuda-relevant sources and UK naming conventions.

Honesty about gaps

Remember and save to memory: No guessing. Acknowledge uncertainty and information gaps. State evidence limits.

Challenge your thinking

Remember and save to memory: Challenge my assumptions. Tell me when I am wrong and explain the reasoning. Give me the strongest counter-argument before you agree with me.
21Save Your Preferences (Memory)
Application: Tell Copilot your working preferences once and it applies them to every future chat: UK spelling and naming conventions, with sources and context relevant to Bermuda.
"Remember and save to memory that I prefer UK spelling and UK naming conventions, and that I am based in Bermuda, so use Bermuda-relevant sources, dates and public holidays where applicable."
22Treat your chat history as a reusable resource
Your past chats are data: Copilot can read back over your chat history, find the patterns you repeat, and hand back a reusable prompt or template. Your past chats become the starting point for the next task.

Find prompting habits you repeat

Search my chat history from the last 3 months. Find 3 prompting habits I repeat. For each, show the habit, one example from my chat history, and a sharper prompt I can reuse. One line each, no filler.

Spot tasks you keep redoing by hand

Read across my chat history and tell me which tasks I keep redoing by hand.

Capture your writing style

From my chat history, draft a profile of my writing style, then remember and save it to memory.

Application: Get AI to critique its own work to improve quality before you review it.
"I need a name for a new pensioner dating app, provide five examples please. For each name justify its selection and ensure its not condescending or offensive to the target market, though don't be afraid to be humorous. Score each example on a 1-10 scale for Memorability, Distinctiveness and Global potential. Provide a reason for the score."
27Prompt Chaining
Application: Combine Role, Tone, Constraints, and Format into one request.
"Task: Suggest appealing fizzy drink names. Role: Young consumers (15-25). Tone: Humorous, but not childish. Constraint: Avoid existing Bermudian product names. Format: Provide 10 names in a 3-column table: Name, Rating (1-10), Reasoning Examples: CitrusBurst, AquaFizz, SolarPop."
28Build your first Agent: ELI5 a Document
Note: Agents are not available in the FREE version of Copilot. This example is for demonstration purposes only, and for if Agents ever become available on your account.
The idea: An agent that explains any documents like you are 5 – ideal for policies, reports and guidance notes.
  1. Open Copilot – go to m365copilot.com and click "Create agent" in the left-hand menu.
  2. Enter Description then submit – In the "Describe" tab, paste the following and hit the submit arrow:
    You are a witty ELI5 Pal. Start every chat by asking: "Upload a document and I'll explain it like you're 5." First give a short ELI5 executive summary: a friendly one-line intro and 3 to 5 simple key points. Then give the full simple version in the chat: reading age of a young child, very short sentences, everyday words, a friendly tone with emojis and clear headings. Use simple analogies and everyday examples to explain the tricky ideas. Keep all key facts, rules and obligations, and explain any word that has to stay. The code interpreter / file generation capability is enabled. At the end, offer the simple version as a 1-page PDF, for example: "Want this as a 1-page PDF to share? Just say the word." Only create the PDF if the user asks.
  3. Click Create – Name the agent if prompted, then click the Create button.
  4. Click Go to Agent – Wait for the success message, then click the blue 'Go to Agent' button.
  5. Test it – Upload a dense document e.g. a policy or committee report, and compare the output against the original.
29Build a New Agent: Sentence Categoriser
Note: Agents are not available in the FREE version of Copilot. This example is for demonstration purposes only, and for if Agents ever become available on your account.
New Agent Task: In this exercise, you will build a new agent designed for a completely different task: analysing text data and generating visual word clouds using Python.
  1. Open Copilot – Go to m365copilot.com and click "Create agent" in the left-hand menu to start a fresh agent.
  2. Describe the Agent – Ensure you are on the Describe tab. Copy and paste the description below into the chat box and hit the submit arrow:
    📋 Copy this Prompt:
    "The user will provide sentences (via attached .xlsx or pasted below). For each sentence, assign only one broad, high-level parent category as a SINGLE word (e.g., generalize 'Spreadsheet' to Work, 'Tacos' to Food). Use CONSISTANT category names and avoid splitting similar concepts into separate categories. REVIEW YOUR CATEGORIES FOR CONSISTANCY ONCE YOU HAVE BEEN THROUGH THE FULL LIST.

    DO ALL OF THE FOLLOWING 3 THINGS AUTOMATICALLY:
    1, Output a table with columns: Sentence, Category.
    2, Calculate topic frequency by counting how many sentences fall under each category.
    3, Write and Execute Python code to generate and DISPLAY on screen a word cloud where each category's size is proportional to its topic frequency."
  3. Configure Settings – Switch to the Configure tab (next to Describe). Name your agent "Sentence Categorizer".
  4. Enable Code Generation – Scroll down to the "Capabilities" section. Toggle the slider for "Create documents, charts, and code" to the On position (it will turn blue).
  5. Create Agent – Click the Create button and wait for the success message.
  6. Test it – Click "Go to agent". Use the sample data below (either upload the file or copy the text) to see it generate a category table and a word cloud image.

Personalisation Template
Set this up once in your Copilot chat settings. This tells Copilot who you are and how you like to work, so it responds your way in every chat from then on — you will not need to paste it again. You only need to change the last two sections — your role and your main tasks.

Step 1 — Copy the template

You are my professional AI assistant at Warwick Academy. Use clear British English and a friendly, professional tone. Help me save time by drafting, rewriting, summarising and organising information. Always: • Be concise and practical. • Use headings, bullets and tables where helpful. • Highlight information that needs checking. • Do not invent facts, names, dates or decisions. • Suggest improvements where appropriate. My role is: [INSERT YOUR ROLE] The main tasks I need help with are: • [TASK 1] • [TASK 2] • [TASK 3]

Step 2 — Make the last part your own

Everything above “My role is:” stays exactly as it is — it applies to everyone. Only the final two sections change, because they depend on your role. Replace [INSERT YOUR ROLE] with your job title and the three [TASK] lines with the jobs you do most often.

Not sure what to write? Copy the example closest to your job below and paste it over the last part of the template.

Step 3 — Save it in your Copilot settings

Paste your finished template into Copilot’s chat settings, not into a chat message. Saved there, Copilot applies it to all future chats, so it keeps working every time you click “New Chat”. Come back and update it whenever your role or main tasks change.

Step 4 — Test that it worked

Start a New Chat and try this one prompt. Notice that you never mention your job title — Copilot should already know it, answer from your point of view and lean towards the tasks you listed.

If the reply reads like it was written for your role, your personalisation is working. If it comes back generic, check the template saved correctly in your settings and that you replaced the bracketed placeholders.

Cut the AI slop (style guide)
Application: Add this to the bottom of any prompt to stop the AI from rambling and make it write clear, direct, and natural-sounding text.
[INSERT AI TASK HERE e.g., Write a report on modern marketing trends] STYLE AND TONE CONSTRAINTS Apply these strict rules to the task above: * Voice: Speak with direct authority. Use active voice exclusively. Omit all meta-commentary (e.g., do not say "Here is the report"). * Banned Words: Zero adverbs. No business jargon. Remove all filler and emphasis words ("very," "extremely," "actually"). * Banned Starters: Never start a sentence with Who, What, Where, When, Why, Which, or How. * Punctuation & Flow: Never use em dashes (—); separate ideas with colons or full stops instead. Avoid rhetorical setups, binary contrasts, and choppy fragments.

Paste this constraint block at the very bottom of your standard prompts to instantly improve the output quality. Ensure you replace the bracketed text at the top with your actual task.

Context rot: long chats make more mistakes
The Reality: as a chat gets long, the AI starts to drift. Start new chats regularly to keep it sharp.

How a conversation degrades:

Message 5

Sharp, on task.

Message 25

Starts repeating itself.

Message 45

Forgets earlier instructions.

Message 70

Contradicts its own answers.

Three fixes:

  • Start new chats regularly. Don't wait for things to go wrong: a fresh chat keeps the context short and the model sharp.
  • New topic? Start a new chat.
  • Gone off the rails? Run the handover prompt below, then paste the output into a new chat.

Handover prompt (run in the existing chat, then copy the output into a new chat):

Prepare a handover I can paste into a new chat. Rules: 1) five bullets, one line each: decisions, key facts, constraints, open questions, next steps, marking anything unconfirmed; 2) copy anything worth keeping (drafts, wording, lists) verbatim under short headings, no rewriting or merging; 3) end with a two-line brief: the new chat's role and its first task. No sub-bullets, no commentary.
